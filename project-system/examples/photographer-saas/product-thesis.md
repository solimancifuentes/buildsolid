## 1. The thesis

The scenario hypothesizes that solo freelance photographers in wedding, portrait, and branded work lose a material share of their time between editing and final client selections. The proposed product is a minimalist delivery SaaS with a private gallery, client-facing delivery link, structured selection inbox, and AI image-suggestion layer that pre-marks frames by sharpness, expression, composition, and near-duplicate culling. Its prospective wedge is time-to-delivered-gallery: the AI suggests, the photographer decides, and the client starts from the photographer-reviewed keeper subset. The named gallery products are descriptive comparison examples, not researched competitors. Nothing else is in the MVP.

## 2. The wedge

The wedge is **time, not features**. The prospective target is for a photographer to hand a 600-image shoot to the product after editing and prepare a delivery-ready gallery in under fifteen minutes of their own time, beginning from explained AI suggestions. The synthetic comparison set contrasts hosted galleries without AI and desktop AI cullers without delivery; it does not establish real competitor capabilities. The proposed whole-loop shape deliberately excludes a sales platform, studio CRM, and Lightroom replacement.

## 3. The audience, in one line

Hypothesized audience: solo, full-time freelance photographers with event-and-portrait-shaped work and roughly 5–40 client galleries per month, for whom selection time may be a meaningful bottleneck.

## 4. Why now

The scenario's timing hypothesis is that vision-language and image-quality models may now make per-frame scoring affordable enough for this narrow workflow while gallery tools remain oriented around hosting. Neither provider pricing nor the competitive premise is established by this example; both require current primary-source validation before a real downstream decision.

## Optional sections

### A. One-line tagline

"A delivery gallery for freelance photographers, where the AI does the first cull and the client sees the keepers first."

### B. What the thesis is **not**

- Not a Lightroom replacement. The product never opens or alters the photographer's RAW files.
- Not a studio-management or invoicing platform. There is no scheduling, no contracts, no payments, no CRM.
- Not a marketplace. The photographer brings their own clients; the product never inserts itself into that relationship.
