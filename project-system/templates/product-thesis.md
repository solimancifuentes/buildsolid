# Product Thesis — Project template

> **Purpose.** A single, compressed paragraph that names what the project is, who it is for, and why it wins. The thesis is the project's elevator pitch to itself — every later artifact is checked against it. Brevity is the point.
>
> **Workflow phase.** Phase 2 — Idea Compression.
>
> **Driving skill.** `idea-compressor`.
>
> **How to use this template.**
> - Replace `<…>` placeholders.
> - The thesis section must end up as **one paragraph**. If you cannot say it in a paragraph, the project has not been compressed yet — go back to [`founder-intent.md`](founder-intent.md) and [`discovery.md`](discovery.md).
> - Keep blockquoted guidance (`>` lines) while drafting; remove or replace before considering the artifact filled.
> - Stable headings — downstream skills rely on them.
> - **Profile applicability.** This artifact is **Required** for New Product Build, **Conditional** for Existing Project Change, and **Optional** for Lightweight/Internal Build unless the product thesis is new or changed.
> - **Section depth.** Numbered sections are required when this artifact is Required, Profile-triggered, or Need-triggered for the selected profile; optional sections stay optional unless project facts make them need-triggered.
> - **Semantic readiness.** This artifact is ready only when it gives a concise thesis, wedge, audience, and timing rationale that later scope and spec artifacts can quote or test. Placeholder cleanup alone is not enough.
> - **Reference example fill:** `project-system/examples/photographer-saas/product-thesis.md`.

Related artifacts: [`founder-intent.md`](founder-intent.md), [`discovery.md`](discovery.md), [`problem-statement.md`](problem-statement.md), [`mvp-scope.md`](mvp-scope.md).

## 1. The thesis

> One paragraph. 4–8 sentences. Includes: (a) the user, (b) the problem they have, (c) what this product is, (d) the wedge — why it wins where the alternatives lose, (e) where the AI layer (if any) is decisive. No marketing copy. No "delight" or "seamless." If you remove a sentence and the meaning still holds, remove it.

## 2. The wedge

> One short paragraph or 3 bullets. The thing this product does that the alternatives in [`discovery.md`](discovery.md) cannot do — or do badly. Be concrete. "Easier UX" is not a wedge; "delivers a final-cut suggestion in under a minute" is.

## 3. The audience, in one line

> One sentence naming the **primary user** in the most specific way that still applies to a real population. This must be consistent with [`founder-intent.md`](founder-intent.md) §2.

## 4. Why now

> 2–4 sentences. What changed in the last 12–36 months that makes this project possible or attractive now? New behavior? New model capability? New cost curve? If "nothing changed", say so honestly — sometimes the answer is just "it was always under-served." Do not invent urgency.

## Optional sections

### A. One-line tagline *(optional)*

> A literal tagline of the form "X for Y that Z." Useful for the eventual landing page; not required for the thesis itself. Skip if it forces marketing-speak.

### B. What the thesis is **not** *(optional)*

> 2–3 bullets distinguishing the thesis from the closest mistaken interpretation. For example, "this is not a marketplace" or "this is not a coaching service." Helps prevent later scope drift; cross-references [`non-goals.md`](non-goals.md) once that exists.
